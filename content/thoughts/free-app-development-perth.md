"Free app developer Perth" gets searched a lot, and I understand why. You've got an idea, maybe a small budget, and app quotes look scary. I'm a freelance app developer in Perth, so I won't pretend I build apps for free. But here's an honest look at the options that do exist, and what each one really costs you.

## Option 1: Build it yourself with no-code tools

Tools like Glide, Adalo, FlutterFlow and Bubble let you build simple apps without writing code, and AI coding assistants have made this much more approachable.

**Good for:** testing an idea, internal tools, simple directories or booking apps.
**The catch:** your time, monthly platform fees, and a ceiling you'll hit if the app takes off. Many founders end up rebuilding properly later, which is fine, as long as you plan for it.

## Option 2: Find a technical co-founder

Some developers will build your app in exchange for equity instead of cash.

**Good for:** a genuinely ambitious startup where you're bringing something big to the table, like customers, industry access or funding.
**The catch:** you're giving away a real share of your company, often 20 to 50 percent. Choose a co-founder the way you'd choose a business partner, because that's what they are.

## Option 3: Student and hackathon projects

Uni capstone projects and hackathons sometimes take on real-world briefs.

**Good for:** non-profits, community projects and experiments.
**The catch:** timelines follow the semester, quality varies and there's usually no one to maintain the app afterwards.

## Option 4: Grants and tax incentives

The federal R&D Tax Incentive can offset part of the cost of genuinely innovative development, and WA government innovation programs open from time to time. Rules and rounds change, so check the current eligibility with an accountant or the program itself.

**Good for:** technically new products, not standard business apps.
**The catch:** paperwork, timing, and you usually spend the money first and claim later.

## Option 5: Spend a little first, before spending a lot

This is what I recommend to most founders. Instead of trying to get the whole app for free, spend a small amount on the step that reduces the most risk:

1. **A clickable prototype.** Real-looking screens you can tap through on your phone, for a fraction of the cost of building. Show it to customers and investors before anyone writes code.
2. **A lean first version.** One problem solved brilliantly, built cross-platform so iPhone and Android come from the same code.
3. **Grow it with real feedback.** Add features your users actually ask for, not the ones you guessed at.

Read my [app development cost guide](/thoughts/app-development-cost-australia) for realistic Australian price ranges at each stage.

## Option 6: Pay monthly

If cash flow is the problem rather than the total cost, ask about payment plans. I offer monthly payments on app projects, so you can launch sooner and pay as the app starts earning.

## What to avoid

- **"Free" builds that keep the code.** Some offers are free up front but lock your app into the developer's account or platform, with fees to leave. Make sure you own the code and the App Store and Google Play accounts.
- **Rock-bottom quotes with no scope.** If a quote seems too good to be true, the missing pieces usually show up as extra invoices later.
- **Building everything at once.** The most expensive app is the one with twenty features nobody uses.
