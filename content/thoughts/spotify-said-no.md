In a not-so-shocking turn of events… Spotify isn’t a big fan of [Musical Drones](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/musical-dones/id6738448757).

They sent me an email.  
Polite. Corporate. Sanitised.

It basically said:  
*Musical Drones doesn’t meet their “business criteria.”*

Translation:  
*“Thanks, but we don’t support satire that points out our billionaire founder invested a billion dollars into killer robots.”*

Fair enough.  
We won’t be supporting theirs either.

And honestly? I think I might print & frame the rejection. It deserves a place of honour in my shed.

### Why This Matters

When I launched this thing, I knew the odds of Spotify approving it were somewhere between slim and none.

*But that was the point.*

Because this whole project isn’t about making a best-in-class fitness app. In fact, it hardly works now that they’ve turned off the integration.  
It’s about holding up a mirror.

To show what happens when the music industry’s most powerful platform isn’t investing in artists, or tools, or community… but in autonomous weapons.

Spotify didn’t just reject my app.  
They rejected accountability.

### What Happens Next

So here’s where we’re at:

- Apple said yes. Musical Drones is [live there](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/musical-dones/id6738448757). So that’s a good start.
- Spotify said no.

And that leaves us with a choice.

Do we keep sending them our money each month, like nothing’s changed?  
Or do we make it clear: if you’re building weapons of war with our subscriptions, we’re out?

### A Bigger Question

Spotify’s rejection letter is almost poetic.

Because it shows, in black and white, that satire and protest don’t “fit” the business model.

But maybe that’s the problem.

When platforms become so big that they decide what voices are “valid” and what questions are “appropriate”… they aren’t just music services anymore. They’re cultural gatekeepers. Gosh, I hate to say it… but I’m starting to sound like X.

But seriously, when those same platforms funnel billions into war tech… they’re something even darker.

### The Party Isn’t Over

So no, Musical Drones won’t be streaming through Spotify playlists.

But the idea behind it doesn’t need their stamp of approval.

The party just moved somewhere else.  
And we found a new DJ to keep the music alive. More on that soon.

Because satire doesn’t need a licence.  
Protest doesn’t need a platform.  
And pointing out hypocrisy doesn’t need anyone’s permission.

### Final Thought

I started this project half as a joke, half as a coping mechanism.  
But every step… every rejection, every email, every shrug from the platforms… makes the joke feel sharper.

Musical Drones is silly, yes.  
But the story it points to? Dead serious.

Spotify said no.  
And in doing so, they gave me the clearest yes yet:

Yes, this conversation matters.  
Yes, they’d rather it didn’t happen.  
Yes, your subscription money really is part of this story.

So maybe the only question left is:

Whose side of history do you want to be streaming on?
