I've designed and built apps that were featured by Apple and an indie marketplace that hit the top 5 in Shopping on the Australian App Store. Founders always ask the same first question: how much? Here's an honest answer.

## Why quotes vary so wildly

You'll hear numbers from a few thousand dollars to well over six figures. Both can be "right." An app is a bundle of decisions, and each one moves the price:

- **Platforms:** iOS, Android or both (cross-platform tools make both cheaper than they used to be)
- **Backend:** user accounts, databases, admin dashboards
- **Payments:** subscriptions, marketplaces, payouts
- **Integrations:** maps, AI models, third-party APIs
- **Design depth:** a few screens or a full design system
- **Who builds it:** offshore team, freelancer, local studio or agency

## Rough ranges

These are broad market ranges, not quotes:

- **Clickable prototype for testing or pitching:** a few thousand dollars
- **Lean MVP from a freelancer or small team:** commonly from the low tens of thousands
- **Full product with custom backend, payments and integrations:** often $50,000 and up
- **Large agency builds:** significantly more

I don't publish a fixed app price on my site for exactly this reason. Every app is different, so I start with a free consult, scope it properly and send a fixed quote you can pay off monthly.

## What AI actually changes

AI coding tools are genuinely speeding up development, and some simple apps really can be built faster and cheaper than a couple of years ago. But the expensive parts of an app were never just typing code: deciding what to build, designing something people love, making it secure, handling edge cases and getting it through App Store review. AI makes good teams faster. It doesn't replace judgement.

## How to spend less (without regretting it)

1. **Start with the one job your app must do brilliantly.** Cut everything else from version one.
2. **Prototype before you build.** Test the idea with a clickable design first.
3. **Go cross-platform** unless you have a real reason not to.
4. **Use existing services** for auth, payments and email instead of building your own.
5. **Plan for after launch.** Updates, hosting and support are ongoing costs.
