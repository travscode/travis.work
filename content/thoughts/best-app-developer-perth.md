Search for "best app developer Perth" and you'll get a wall of agencies all claiming to be the best. I'm a freelance app developer in Perth myself, so take this with that in mind, but after 20+ years in agencies and studios and apps featured by Apple, here's how I'd choose if I were in your shoes.

## 1. Download their apps

Portfolio mockups are easy to make. Live apps are not. Ask for links to apps the developer actually built and launched, then download them and use them for five minutes. Do they feel smooth? Are they still being updated? Are the reviews decent?

If a developer can't point you to a single app you can install, that tells you a lot.

## 2. Find out who actually does the work

With agencies, the person who wins your project is often not the person who builds it. Ask directly:

- Who designs the app?
- Who writes the code, and are they in-house, contracted or offshore?
- Who do I talk to when something goes wrong?

None of these answers is automatically bad. You just want to know before you sign.

## 3. Get a fixed scope, not a vague estimate

A good app developer will push back on your feature list and help you cut it down to the version worth launching first. Be wary of anyone who happily quotes everything you asked for without asking why. Look for:

- A written scope of what's in version one (and what isn't)
- A fixed price or clear stage-by-stage pricing
- A timeline with check-ins where you get to use the real app on your phone

If you're still working out a budget, my [app development cost guide](/thoughts/app-development-cost-australia) has realistic Australian ranges.

## 4. Make sure you own everything

This one catches people out. Your App Store and Google Play developer accounts should be in your business's name, and you should get the full source code. If a developer insists on publishing the app under their own account, you could find yourself unable to update your own app later.

## 5. Ask what happens after launch

Apps aren't finished at launch. Apple and Google change their requirements every year, phones get new screen sizes and your users will ask for features. Ask how updates are handled, what they cost and how fast fixes happen.

## Freelancer, agency or offshore?

- **Freelance app developer:** Direct contact, lower overheads, fast decisions. Best for startups, MVPs and small business apps. Make sure they cover design and development, or have trusted people who do.
- **Perth agency:** More people, more process and more cost. Worth it for large apps that need several specialists working in parallel.
- **Offshore team:** Lowest hourly rates. Can work well with a strong local product owner, but miscommunication and rework often eat the savings.

I've written more about this trade-off in [freelancer vs agency](/thoughts/freelancer-vs-agency-website). It's about websites, but the same logic applies to apps.

## Red flags

- No live apps you can download
- A quote that arrives before anyone has asked about your users
- Vague answers about who owns the code
- "We can build anything" with no pushback on scope
- No plan for App Store and Google Play review
